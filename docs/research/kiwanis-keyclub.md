## 2. Tools Kiwanis and Key Club actually use

Evidence here is anecdotal, drawn from individual school chapter pages, not from official Kiwanis guidance.

- Google Sheets and Google Forms are the most common hour tracking approach. Lambert High School keeps hours in a Google Sheet maintained by advisors and officers and uses check-in sheets or Google Forms at events (https://lambertkeyclub.onuniverse.com/faq/how-are-my-hours-tracked). Another chapter uses a per-event Google Form so members claim their own hours (https://hs.cvschools.org/athletics___extracurriculars/key_club/general_information/ was returned for this theme; the specific form detail is from the search summary).
- GroupMe is used for officer and member chatter, for example members are told to message an officer there about hours (same Lambert page, and https://sites.google.com/view/glenelgkeyclub/faq).
- SignUpGenius is widely used for shift sign-up and has a Remind integration (https://www.signupgenius.com/blog/signupgenius-integrated-with-remind.cfm). A Capterra comparison reports it has no time-tracking feature (https://www.capterra.com/compare/135392-174383/SignUpGenius-vs-Helper-Helper).
- Dedicated platforms that Key Club chapters adopt: Track It Forward (https://trackitforward.com/join/high-school-service-clubs) and Helper Helper (https://www.helperhelper.com/?p=21309). Both combine event sign-up and hour logging, which is the gap SignUpGenius leaves.
- Key Club International also runs a Membership Update Center for rosters and dues; a service hour only counts for a rostered, dues-paid member (https://www.newenglandkeyclub.org/resources/what-is-a-service-hour).
- Email lists, Remind and ClubRunner are plausible for the adult Kiwanis side, but I found no source confirming any of them for Campbell. UNVERIFIED. I also did not confirm what Kiwanis International member tools Campbell uses.

Takeaway: the real competitor is a free combination of Google Form plus GroupMe plus a spreadsheet, plus the friction of chasing replies. Any product has to be easier than a group chat.

## 3. Service hours, verification, and who the users are

- Key Club International Policy Code defines a service hour as an hour of service by an individual member with the approval of the club board of directors, performed for the community by a rostered member with paid dues (https://www.newenglandkeyclub.org/resources/what-is-a-service-hour).
- Hour requirements are set locally and vary widely. Examples found range from 15 hours per semester to 40 or 50 hours per year (https://sites.google.com/wcpss.net/athensdrivekeyclub/faqs, https://sites.google.com/view/glenelgkeyclub/faq). Requirements for Campbell-area clubs are UNVERIFIED.
- Verification is mostly local paperwork. One school hours sheet must be signed by the event coordinator or an advisor only (https://slhs.pasco.k12.fl.us/wp-content/uploads/slhs/2023/07/2023-24-Key-Club-Hours-Sheet.pdf). Another club says hours without a signature or documentation do not count, and accepts a printed organizer email for virtual events. The CNH district secretary manual (https://www.cnhkeyclub.org/images/Resources/Club%20Officers/Secretary/2021-2022-secretary-manual.pdf) covers monthly reporting; I did not read it, so the exact monthly report format is UNVERIFIED. The CNH training deck warns against inflating or doubling hours and lists advisor approval as a project-planning step.
- Demographics: Key Club members are high school students, so they are reachable by text and chat and are on phones all day. Kiwanis adult members skew older, which is common knowledge for service clubs but I found no Campbell-specific membership data. UNVERIFIED for Campbell.

Design implications (my inference, not sourced):

- Tap-a-link with no account is the right call for both groups. Teens will not create another account for one event, and older members will not remember a password.
- SMS reaches both groups with the least friction. Email is better for older members and for anything an advisor needs to keep as a record (the "printed organizer email" documentation noted above). Offering both, per person, is safer than picking one.
- A coordinator-signed confirmation (a verification line the organizer can tap or sign) would map directly onto the advisor/coordinator signature that Key Clubs already require for hours.

## 4. Privacy and minors

- Kiwanis International Youth Protection policy is the most relevant constraint. The 2025 materials call for at least two adults on electronic communication with youth (a background-checked advisor, a chaperone, or the student's parent), avoiding one-on-one adult-to-student texts and emails, setting set times of day for messages, and connecting through club pages rather than personal accounts (https://www.kiwanis.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-Youth-Protection-Slide-Deck.pdf and https://www.kiwanis.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/YOUTH-PROTECTION-POLICIES-PROCEDURES.pdf). Older documents differ in wording, and I did not locate the full current policy text, so confirm with the club's youth protection contact. Practical consequence: any text blast from a Kiwanis adult directly to a teen's personal phone is exactly what this policy discourages. A tool that sends from a shared club number, or copies the advisor, is more defensible than an adult's personal phone.
- COPPA applies to children under 13, so Key Club members (high school) are generally outside it. This is my knowledge, and the FTC FAQ is at https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20201219104030/https://www.ftc.gov/tips-advice/business-center/guidance/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions. Younger Builders Club or K-Kids participants would be a different story, and are out of scope.
- School and district policy: districts typically treat student phone numbers as sensitive and limit release absent parental consent, and clubs on campus are usually advisor-supervised. I did not find the Campbell Union High School District or Campbell Union School District rules. UNVERIFIED.
- California law, from my own knowledge and not confirmed in this research: the CCPA/CPRA requires opt-in consent before selling or sharing personal data of consumers under 16, and the state student-privacy statutes (SOPIPA, AB 1584) mainly constrain operators serving schools, not a community club. Texting also needs consent under the TCPA. Check all of this with someone qualified before shipping anything beyond a demo.
- Safe hackathon posture: collect the minimum (first name, a contact, a yes/no), store no teen data you do not need, use fake data in the demo, never use real student phone numbers, and make opt-out one tap.

## 5. Five questions for the Kiwanis partner at the event

1. Walk me through the last event you staffed: who asked whom, through what channel, and where did it go wrong or take the most time?
2. Who actually does the chasing and the final headcount, and how many hours a week does that take? Is it one person or several?
3. How do you reach Key Club members and adult members today (text, email, GroupMe, Remind, SignUpGenius, paper), and who would you not be able to reach with a text-only tool?
4. What do advisors or coordinators need to sign or send so a student's service hours count, and would a tap-to-confirm record that satisfies them?
5. What are your youth protection rules for messaging students, and who must be copied on messages, so we respect them from the start?
